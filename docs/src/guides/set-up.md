# Set up

About five minutes, on your phone, right after [installing](./index.md). You give the device the
name of your guest Wi-Fi, choose who may join it, and choose your owner password. At the end you
join the management Wi-Fi the device made for you.

## Before you start

- Your phone is joined to the setup Wi-Fi, `Enkrnett Setup` followed by the four characters from
  the device's label.
- Have a pen or the screenshot button ready: two things must be kept, and the device shows them
  only once.
- The settings screen is at `http://192.168.207.1/`. Your phone's browser may show a warning
  beside the address because the page has no web certificate: the page is delivered inside the
  device's own Wi-Fi, and nothing leaves the room.

## The steps

The screen says *Step n of 6* (or of 7 on a device sold in more than one country) and asks one
thing at a time.

1. **Set up this device.** The first line tells you whether your phone joined the setup Wi-Fi with
   encryption. On an older phone it may say your phone joined without encryption and that someone
   within range could read what you enter: then set up with a newer phone, or at a quieter time.
   Press *Start*.
2. **Is this the device?** Press *Blink* and look at the device: its light blinks. Press *I saw it
   blink*. If you have more than one device, this step tells you which one you are setting up.
3. **Country or region.** Only on a device sold in more than one country. Wi-Fi rules differ by
   country; choose where the device is used. This is the one choice that only Erase can change.
4. **Guest Wi-Fi.** Type the name your guests will see on their phones, for example your shop's
   name. The screen counts what is left: up to 32 characters, and Japanese and other full-width
   characters count as 3 each, because a Wi-Fi name has a fixed size. Then choose who can join:
   - *Open to every device*: devices that can encrypt do; older devices connect without
     encryption. In this choice the name can use at most 29.
   - *Encrypted only*: every connection is encrypted; older devices cannot join.
   You can change both later.
5. **Owner password.** 10 to 64 characters: letters, numbers and symbols you can type on any
   keyboard. Not a Wi-Fi name, not the management Wi-Fi password, not a common password. You enter
   it whenever you change anything. **Keep it somewhere safe: if it is lost, the only way back is
   Erase, and you set up again.**
6. **Keep these.** The device shows the name of your management Wi-Fi, its password, and the
   address of the settings screen, with two codes to scan. Write them down or take a screenshot:
   this phone cannot scan its own screen. Then press *Set up*.
7. **Join the management Wi-Fi.** The setup Wi-Fi is closing. Open your phone's Wi-Fi settings,
   join the management Wi-Fi with the password you kept, and come back to the browser; the screen
   continues by itself. You have ten minutes. If the ten minutes pass without you logging in, the
   device forgets everything and offers the setup Wi-Fi again; nothing is lost but five minutes.
8. **Log in** with the owner password. The *Status* screen appears: your guest Wi-Fi is running.

## Afterwards

- **Status** shows the guest Wi-Fi's name, how many devices are connected and how many of them
  are encrypted, the management Wi-Fi, and the Internet connection.
- **What this protects** (the link under the counts) says in plain words what is encrypted and
  what is not, and gives you a notice to print for your guests.
- **Settings** (the menu) changes the guest Wi-Fi's name, who can join, on or off, and the
  management Wi-Fi's name. Every change asks for nothing more than *Save*; a change to the
  management Wi-Fi asks you to join it again within ten minutes, or it is undone.
- **Management Wi-Fi** (the menu) shows the password again when you ask, with the code to scan
  for a second phone, and lets you replace it.

## Two passwords, two jobs

| | Who uses it | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Management Wi-Fi password | Every phone that should reach the settings screen | Joins the management Wi-Fi |
| Owner password | You | Changes anything on the settings screen |

A phone on the management Wi-Fi without the owner password sees the login screen and nothing
else. Your guests see neither: the guest Wi-Fi and the management Wi-Fi are kept apart by the
device.
